The Story in Brief

Struggling writer Lowen Ashby (Dakota Johnson) accepts a commission to ghostwrite the remaining books in a bestselling thriller series, working from the home of the incapacitated author, Verity Crawford (Anne Hathaway). In the house, she discovers an unpublished manuscript — an autobiography so dark and detailed that it may be a confession to something terrible. Lowen must decide whether what she's reading is truth or fiction, and whether the man she's falling for (Josh Hartnett) knows what his wife has done.

Core Differences

The central ambiguity

The novel's architecture is built on a single question: is the manuscript a genuine confession or an elaborate fiction? Hoover gives Lowen exactly the information needed to make a case either way. The film makes this ambiguity slightly harder to sustain; the camera has to commit to a visual register that prose can leave to the imagination.

The reveal timing

The film moves the central revelation approximately fifteen minutes earlier than the novel does. This changes the shape of the final act — the last quarter of the book operates differently when you don't know, compared to when you do. The film's restructuring is propulsive but alters the visceral experience of the mystery.

Verdict

Both versions are exercises in controlled discomfort. The novel has the edge because its ambiguity is purer — prose allows for a level of doubt that the camera lens often clarifies. However, the 2026 adaptation is a masterclass in tension. Read first, then watch.
