What’s working

The concept is clear immediately: comparing books and screen adaptations.

The site feels easy to scan because it is organized by genre.

The homepage has strong breadth, with many recognizable titles that give it instant credibility.

The tone is simple and approachable, which fits a recommendation/review style site.

What needs improvement

The homepage is very long and repetitive, so the most important content can get lost.

There is no obvious “why this site is different” beyond the title and short intro.

The page uses many list-style entries, but there is little guidance on where to start.

The site would likely benefit from stronger featured content, search, or sorting.

Some spacing and hierarchy could be tightened so the page feels more polished and less like a catalog.

Go-forward advice

Add a short “Start here” section at the top with 3 to 5 featured comparisons.

Add a one-sentence explanation of your method, such as how you judge whether the book or movie is better.

Create a clear reading path: “Best adaptations,” “Most different adaptations,” “Most overrated movies,” or “Best books to read first.”

Reduce repetition by grouping content more tightly and avoiding multiple genre labels unless they add real value.

Add internal links for each title so users can click into a dedicated page with a full comparison.

Consider adding ratings or a simple verdict badge, such as “Book wins,” “Movie wins,” or “Too close to call.”

Add a search bar or filters for genre, mood, length, and whether the user should read the book first.

Make the homepage highlight the strongest 6 to 10 entries instead of trying to showcase everything equally.

Add a short about page so visitors know who is writing the comparisons and why they should trust them.

Include basic SEO improvements like better page titles, meta descriptions, and descriptive headings.

Highest-priority next steps

Rewrite the top of the homepage so it explains the site in 2 to 4 sentences and points users to featured comparisons.

Turn each book/movie pair into a proper article page.

Add a simple verdict system and keep it consistent across the site.

Improve navigation so the site is useful as a guide, not just a list.

Child page example: People We Meet on Vacation
Before
The page likely does too much explaining before it gets to the actual comparison.

The title and on-page copy are probably not centered on a single search intent, like “People We Meet on Vacation book vs movie.”

The page may not clearly surface the verdict, comparison categories, or a clean summary near the top.

After
Make the page answer one main question immediately: is the book or adaptation better?

Put the verdict near the top.

Use predictable sections so both readers and search engines can understand the page quickly.

Include one short summary paragraph, then comparison sections like plot, characters, pacing, faithfulness, and recommendation.
