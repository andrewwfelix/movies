    <section id="faq">
        <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

        <h3>Is Lonesome Dove based on a true story?</h3>
        <p><strong>No, Lonesome Dove is not based on a true story.</strong> However, Larry McMurtry drew heavily on historical research about the real American West, cattle drives, and Texas Rangers. The characters are fictional, but the historical setting and many events are grounded in reality. McMurtry was inspired by real cattle drives like the one led by Charles Goodnight and Oliver Loving.</p>

        <h3>What genre is Lonesome Dove?</h3>
        <p><strong>Western / Historical Fiction.</strong> More specifically, it's a revisionist Western that deconstructs romantic myths about the Old West while still honoring the genre's traditions. It combines elements of adventure, drama, and literary fiction.</p>

        <h3>What year did Lonesome Dove come out?</h3>
        <p><strong>Book:</strong> 1985<br>
        <strong>Miniseries:</strong> 1989</p>

        <h3>How long is Lonesome Dove?</h3>
        <p><strong>Book:</strong> 945 pages (varies by edition)<br>
        <strong>Miniseries:</strong> Approximately 6 hours across 4 episodes</p>

        <h3>Should I read the book or watch the miniseries first?</h3>
        <p><strong>Either works well.</strong> The miniseries is remarkably faithful, so watching it won't spoil the book's pleasures. Many fans recommend watching the miniseries first (it's more accessible), then reading the book for deeper character insight and additional storylines.</p>

        <h3>Are there other Lonesome Dove adaptations?</h3>
        <p>Yes, CBS produced three additional miniseries based on McMurtry's other novels:</p>
        <ul>
            <li><strong>Return to Lonesome Dove</strong> (1993) - sequel miniseries</li>
            <li><strong>Streets of Laredo</strong> (1995) - based on McMurtry's sequel novel</li>
            <li><strong>Dead Man's Walk</strong> (1996) - prequel</li>
            <li><strong>Comanche Moon</strong> (2008) - prequel</li>
        </ul>
        <p>However, none achieved the critical acclaim or cultural impact of the original 1989 miniseries.</p>

        <h3>Why is it called "Lonesome Dove"?</h3>
        <p><strong>Lonesome Dove is the name of the small Texas border town</strong> where Gus and Call run their ranch. The name evokes the isolation, melancholy, and fading frontier spirit that permeates the story.</p>

        <h3>Is Lonesome Dove suitable for children?</h3>
        <p><strong>No, it's intended for mature audiences.</strong> Both book and miniseries contain graphic violence, sexual content, racial themes, and intense emotional situations. It's generally recommended for ages 16+.</p>

        <h3>Where can I watch the Lonesome Dove miniseries?</h3>
        <p>The miniseries is available on various streaming platforms (availability changes), and on DVD/Blu-ray. Check current streaming services for availability.</p>

        <h3>Did Larry McMurtry write the miniseries screenplay?</h3>
        <p><strong>No.</strong> Bill Wittliff adapted the novel for television. However, McMurtry was involved in the production and approved of the adaptation.</p>
