What is Lonesome Dove?

Lonesome Dove is both a Pulitzer Prize-winning novel by Larry McMurtry (published 1985) and a critically acclaimed television miniseries (aired 1989). It's an epic Western that follows two retired Texas Rangers on a cattle drive from Texas to Montana in the 1870s.

At a Glance

  • Genre: Western, Historical Fiction
  • Book Published: 1985
  • Miniseries Aired: February 5-8, 1989 (CBS)
  • Setting: 1870s American West (Texas to Montana)
  • Awards: Pulitzer Prize for Fiction (1986), 7 Emmy Awards

The Book: Larry McMurtry's Pulitzer Prize Winner

Publication & Reception

Published in 1985 by Simon & Schuster, Lonesome Dove won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction in 1986. The 945-page novel is considered one of the greatest Western novels ever written and revitalized the Western genre in American literature.

What Makes the Book Special

  • Epic scope: Nearly 1,000 pages covering multiple storylines and dozens of characters
  • Literary depth: Complex character psychology and philosophical themes about aging, friendship, and the dying West
  • Historical detail: Meticulously researched period accuracy
  • Subversion of Western tropes: McMurtry deconstructs romanticized Western myths

The Lonesome Dove Book Series

McMurtry wrote four additional novels in the Lonesome Dove series:

  1. Dead Man's Walk (1995) - Prequel
  2. Comanche Moon (1997) - Prequel
  3. Lonesome Dove (1985) - Original
  4. Streets of Laredo (1993) - Sequel
  5. Return to Lonesome Dove (1993) - Sequel (note: McMurtry did not write this)

The Miniseries: The 1989 Television Event

Production Details

  • Network: CBS
  • Air Dates: February 5-8, 1989
  • Episodes: 4 parts (6 hours total runtime)
  • Director: Simon Wincer
  • Screenwriter: Bill Wittliff

Cast

  • Robert Duvall as Augustus "Gus" McCrae
  • Tommy Lee Jones as Woodrow F. Call
  • Danny Glover as Joshua Deets
  • Diane Lane as Lorena Wood
  • Robert Urich as Jake Spoon
  • Anjelica Huston as Clara Allen
  • Ricky Schroder as Newt Dobbs

Critical Acclaim & Awards

The miniseries was a massive critical and commercial success:

  • Won 7 Emmy Awards (including Outstanding Miniseries)
  • Won 2 Golden Globe Awards
  • Averaged 26 million viewers per night
  • Considered one of the greatest Western films/miniseries ever made

Story & Synopsis

The Basic Plot

Lonesome Dove follows former Texas Rangers Augustus "Gus" McCrae and Woodrow F. Call, who run a small ranch and livery in the dusty Texas border town of Lonesome Dove. When an old friend suggests a cattle drive to Montana—where land is plentiful and unsettled—Call seizes the opportunity for one last adventure.

The Journey

The story chronicles their epic 2,500-mile cattle drive from Texas to Montana, encountering:

  • Hostile Comanche and outlaw bands
  • Treacherous river crossings and stampedes
  • Harsh weather and unforgiving terrain
  • Personal demons and past regrets
  • Love, loss, and the cost of the frontier life

Major Themes

  • Friendship: The complex, decades-long bond between Gus and Call
  • The dying West: The end of the frontier era and the cowboy way of life
  • Masculinity and violence: The cost of the violent frontier code
  • Unfulfilled dreams: Characters haunted by roads not taken
  • Mortality: Aging men facing their final adventures

Book vs Miniseries: Key Differences

What the Miniseries Changed

1. Condensed Storylines

Book: Multiple subplots and dozens of supporting characters with full arcs
Miniseries: Streamlined to focus on main characters; some subplots reduced or eliminated

2. Character Depth

Book: Extensive internal monologues and psychological complexity
Miniseries: Character psychology conveyed through performance and dialogue; some nuance lost

3. Violence & Brutality

Book: Graphic, unflinching depictions of frontier violence
Miniseries: Toned down for television but still intense for 1989 standards

4. Lorena's Storyline

Book: More graphic and traumatic captivity sequence
Miniseries: Abbreviated and less explicit while maintaining emotional impact

5. Pacing

Book: Leisurely, episodic pacing with room for digressions
Miniseries: More compressed timeline; some events reordered for dramatic effect

What the Miniseries Got Right

  • Casting: Duvall and Jones perfectly embody Gus and Call
  • Tone: Captures the elegiac, melancholy spirit of the novel
  • Key scenes: Major moments (Gus's death, the final reunion) are faithfully adapted
  • Dialogue: Much of McMurtry's dialogue preserved verbatim
  • Visual authenticity: Period-accurate costumes, locations, and production design

The Verdict: Book vs Miniseries

The miniseries is one of the most faithful and successful literary adaptations ever made. While it necessarily condenses the novel's sprawling scope, it captures the essential spirit, themes, and emotional core. Fans generally agree both are masterpieces in their respective mediums.

Historical Setting & Timeline

When is Lonesome Dove Set?

Time Period: Approximately 1876-1877 (the book is somewhat vague on exact dates)

Historical Context

The story takes place during a pivotal moment in American history:

  • Post-Civil War: About 11-12 years after the war's end
  • Indian Wars era: Ongoing conflicts with Comanche and other tribes
  • Open range period: Before barbed wire fenced the West
  • Cattle drive boom: Peak era of long-distance cattle drives
  • Frontier closing: The beginning of the end of the "Wild West"

Geographic Journey

The cattle drive covers approximately 2,500 miles:

  1. Start: Lonesome Dove, Texas (fictional town on the Rio Grande)
  2. Through: San Antonio, Austin, Fort Worth
  3. Across: Indian Territory (Oklahoma), Kansas, Nebraska
  4. End: Montana Territory (near present-day Miles City)

The Real West vs Lonesome Dove

McMurtry drew on extensive historical research, incorporating real elements:

  • Actual cattle drive routes and practices
  • Historical events (though characters are fictional)
  • Authentic period details about frontier life
  • Real geography and place names

Main Characters

Augustus "Gus" McCrae

Former Texas Ranger, now co-owner of the Hat Creek Cattle Company. Philosophical, romantic, and garrulous. Haunted by lost love Clara Allen. The heart of the story.

Woodrow F. Call

Former Texas Ranger captain, Gus's lifelong friend and partner. Stoic, driven, emotionally repressed. Obsessed with duty and legacy. The conscience of the story.

Joshua Deets

Former slave and longtime companion to Gus and Call. Legendary tracker and scout. Loyal, wise, and indispensable to the outfit.

Lorena Wood ("Lorie")

Prostitute in Lonesome Dove who dreams of San Francisco. Becomes involved with Jake Spoon, then endures trauma before finding redemption.

Jake Spoon

Charming but weak former Ranger. His arrival in Lonesome Dove sets the cattle drive in motion, but his poor choices lead to tragedy.

Newt Dobbs

Young ranch hand, likely Call's unacknowledged son. Represents innocence and the next generation.

Clara Allen

Gus's former love, now married and running a ranch in Nebraska. Represents the life and love Gus gave up for the Ranger life.

Blue Duck

Renegade Comanche warrior and the story's primary antagonist. Represents the violence and brutality of the frontier.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Lonesome Dove based on a true story?

No, Lonesome Dove is not based on a true story. However, Larry McMurtry drew heavily on historical research about the real American West, cattle drives, and Texas Rangers. The characters are fictional, but the historical setting and many events are grounded in reality. McMurtry was inspired by real cattle drives like the one led by Charles Goodnight and Oliver Loving.

What genre is Lonesome Dove?

Western / Historical Fiction. More specifically, it's a revisionist Western that deconstructs romantic myths about the Old West while still honoring the genre's traditions. It combines elements of adventure, drama, and literary fiction.

What year did Lonesome Dove come out?

Book: 1985
Miniseries: 1989

How long is Lonesome Dove?

Book: 945 pages (varies by edition)
Miniseries: Approximately 6 hours across 4 episodes

Should I read the book or watch the miniseries first?

Either works well. The miniseries is remarkably faithful, so watching it won't spoil the book's pleasures. Many fans recommend watching the miniseries first (it's more accessible), then reading the book for deeper character insight and additional storylines.

Are there other Lonesome Dove adaptations?

Yes, CBS produced three additional miniseries based on McMurtry's other novels:

  • Return to Lonesome Dove (1993) - sequel miniseries
  • Streets of Laredo (1995) - based on McMurtry's sequel novel
  • Dead Man's Walk (1996) - prequel
  • Comanche Moon (2008) - prequel

However, none achieved the critical acclaim or cultural impact of the original 1989 miniseries.

Why is it called "Lonesome Dove"?

Lonesome Dove is the name of the small Texas border town where Gus and Call run their ranch. The name evokes the isolation, melancholy, and fading frontier spirit that permeates the story.

Is Lonesome Dove suitable for children?

No, it's intended for mature audiences. Both book and miniseries contain graphic violence, sexual content, racial themes, and intense emotional situations. It's generally recommended for ages 16+.

Where can I watch the Lonesome Dove miniseries?

The miniseries is available on various streaming platforms (availability changes), and on DVD/Blu-ray. Check current streaming services for availability.

Did Larry McMurtry write the miniseries screenplay?

No. Bill Wittliff adapted the novel for television. However, McMurtry was involved in the production and approved of the adaptation.

Final Thoughts: Why Lonesome Dove Endures

More than 35 years after the book's publication and the miniseries' broadcast, Lonesome Dove remains a cultural touchstone. It succeeds because it's simultaneously:

  • A rollicking adventure story
  • A meditation on friendship, aging, and mortality
  • A historically grounded portrait of the American West
  • A deconstruction of Western mythology

Whether you experience it through McMurtry's prose or the miniseries' performances, Lonesome Dove offers a powerful, moving journey through one of America's defining eras.
